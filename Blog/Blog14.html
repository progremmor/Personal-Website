From Building a Bocce Court to Building a Nonprofit: A Year of Growth and Gratitude

August 15, 2026



How It Started

When I started my Eagle Scout service project 2 years ago, I thought the biggest challenge would be building a bocce court for the Lexington Community Center. What I didn't realize was that the project would teach me lessons that would shape everything I would do afterward.

The bocce court project required much more than construction. I had to plan every detail, create a budget, raise funds, rent tools, coordinate volunteers, solve unexpected shipping and weather problems, and adapt when things did not go as planned. There were moments when I had to pivot, rethink my approach, and find creative solutions to keep the project moving forward. Looking back, that experience gave me something even more valuable than the completed court. It gave me confidence. That confidence became the foundation for launching CodeForward.


Growth of CodeForward

When I first founded CodeForward, I never imagined it would grow into what it is today. What started as a simple idea, to make coding and technology education more accessible, has become an opportunity to reach students both locally and across the country. Every workshop, every volunteer, and every student who joins reminds me how powerful technology can be when combined with a passion for helping others.

This summer, I was excited to partner with the Lexington Community Center to offer a free 2-week coding workshop for local students. Originally planned as a combination of 1-week website development and 1-week Python programming, the workshop was adjusted based on student enthusiasm and feedback into a 2-week web development program. Students learned how the internet works and built their own websites using HTML and CSS through live virtual sessions and hands-on projects.

It was incredibly rewarding to watch students go from having little or no coding experience to proudly sharing websites they created themselves. Adapting the workshop to their interests made the experience even more engaging and meaningful.

Perhaps the biggest surprise was seeing parents join the learning journey! What began as a program for students quickly became a family activity, with parents following website development lessons, asking questions, and exploring technology alongside their children. Watching families learn, create, and grow together through STEM was both unexpected and inspiring, reminding me that curiosity has no age limit.


Final Thoughts

I see many parallels between the students in our workshops and my younger self during my Eagle Scout project. Both experiences start with uncertainty, involve overcoming challenges, and build confidence through small successes.

I'm grateful to our volunteers, community partners, families, and especially the students who embraced the challenge of learning something new. The bocce court taught me how to lead. CodeForward has shown me how much impact that leadership can have when it's used to serve others.

What I once thought was a small idea has grown beyond anything I expected, and I'm excited to continue helping more students discover the confidence that comes from creating, learning, and solving problems.
